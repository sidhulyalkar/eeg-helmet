# Community conduct and research responsibility

NeuroShield welcomes students, builders, researchers, patient advocates, accessibility reviewers and independent contributors regardless of geography, training, resources, gender, disability, race or institutional affiliation. People should be able to challenge a scientific claim without being attacked personally.

## Expected behavior

- Discuss evidence, limitations, methods and results precisely and respectfully.
- Assume contributors may be new to the technical or scientific context; explain jargon and invite questions.
- Credit original projects and contributors; clearly distinguish inspiration, independent implementation and licensed reuse.
- Be receptive to peer review, including negative results and corrections of an overclaim.
- Make documentation usable with accessible formats, clear terminology and setup paths that do not require proprietary software or hardware.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, stalking, sexualized remarks, abusive or discriminatory language, retaliation, doxxing or unwanted contact.
- Posting another person's EEG, MRI/head scans, health information, identity or private communications without an appropriate authorized process.
- Encouraging unsafe human-device experiments, circumventing consent, or representing unvalidated equipment as protective or medically approved.
- Pressuring maintainers to suppress replication failures, falsify tests, manufacture approvals or endorse scientific claims without evidence.

## Scope and enforcement

This applies to repositories, discussions, code reviews, project events and official collaboration spaces. Maintainers may edit/remove harmful content, request changes, restrict access or ban participants depending on severity. Decisions should be documented without exposing private reports; people involved should have a reasonable opportunity to respond when safe.

**Private reporting setup is a launch blocker:** this repository does not yet publish a dedicated, confirmed confidential conduct-reporting route. Until maintainers establish and test one, do not submit sensitive incident details or participant records in public GitHub issues. For abuse occurring on GitHub itself, use [GitHub's reporting mechanisms](https://support.github.com/contact/report-abuse). Maintainers should add a verified private contact mechanism and clearly document response/appeal procedures before organizing broader hosted community activities.

The code of conduct is a community participation policy, **not** an ethics approval, human-research consent process, safety certification or legal waiver. See [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) for security-reporting limitations, [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) for scientific changes, and [the ecosystem blueprint](docs/open-eeg-ecosystem-and-community.md) for governance proposals.
